SECTION 1: WHAT IS PHISHING?
================================================================
Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where a cybercriminal
disguises a malicious message as a trustworthy one. The goal is to
trick the recipient into:
  • Clicking a harmful link
  • Downloading malware
  • Giving away passwords, OTPs, or financial details

Phishing is responsible for over 90% of data breaches worldwide.
Understanding how to identify phishing emails is a fundamental
cybersecurity skill.


SECTION 2: PHISHING IDENTIFICATION CHECKLIST
================================================================
Use this checklist every time you receive a suspicious email.

[ ] 1. SENDER ADDRESS
        Does the "From" address match the real organization domain?
        e.g., "support@paypa1.com" instead of "support@paypal.com"
        (notice the digit "1" replacing the letter "l")

[ ] 2. URGENT / THREATENING LANGUAGE
        Does the email pressure you with phrases like:
        "Act NOW!", "Your account will be SUSPENDED in 24 hours!",
        "Immediate action required"?
        Legitimate companies rarely demand instant action.

[ ] 3. GENERIC GREETING
        Does the email say "Dear Customer" instead of your real name?
        Companies that know you will use your actual name.

[ ] 4. MISMATCHED / SUSPICIOUS LINKS
        Hover over any link (don't click!) — does the URL shown at
        the bottom of the screen match what's written in the email?
        e.g., link text says "www.amazon.com" but URL is
        "http://amaz0n-deals.ru/login"

[ ] 5. SPELLING & GRAMMAR ERRORS
        Many phishing emails originate from non-English speakers.
        Look for awkward grammar, random capitalization, or typos.

[ ] 6. UNEXPECTED ATTACHMENTS
        Did you expect this file? .exe, .zip, .docm attachments
        are especially dangerous — they can contain malware.

[ ] 7. REQUESTS FOR SENSITIVE INFORMATION
        Legitimate banks and services NEVER ask for your password,
        OTP, or card details via email.

[ ] 8. TOO-GOOD-TO-BE-TRUE OFFERS
        "You've won a $1,000 Amazon gift card — claim now!"
        These are designed to override your critical thinking.

[ ] 9. DOMAIN SPOOFING IN DISPLAY NAME
        The display name might say "PayPal Support" but the actual
        email is "noreply@random-server.xyz".

[ ] 10. MISSING HTTPS / SECURE PADLOCK
        Any link asking for login credentials should start with
        "https://" — if it's "http://", leave immediately.


SECTION 3: SAMPLE PHISHING EMAILS WITH RED FLAGS MARKED
================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------
SAMPLE EMAIL 1 — "Account Suspended" Phishing
-------------------------------------------------------------------
From:    security@paypa1.com                 ← [🚩 FAKE DOMAIN: "1" not "l"]
To:      you@email.com
Subject: ⚠️ Your Account Has Been SUSPENDED!  ← [🚩 URGENT LANGUAGE]

Dear Customer,                               ← [🚩 GENERIC GREETING]

We noticed suspicious activity on your PayPal account.
Your account has been SUSPENDED. You must verify your
identity within 24 hours or your account will be DELETED. ← [🚩 THREAT + URGENCY]

Click here to verify: http://paypa1-secure.xyz/verify   ← [🚩 FAKE URL / NO HTTPS]

Enter your:
  • Username and password                    ← [🚩 REQUESTING CREDENTIALS]
  • Credit card number
  • OTP from your bank

Failure to act will result in permanent closure.

PayPal Security Team

RED FLAGS FOUND: 5
  1. Sender domain: "paypa1.com" (digit 1 instead of L)
  2. Urgent subject line with capitalization
  3. Generic "Dear Customer" greeting
  4. Threatening language ("account will be DELETED")
  5. Suspicious link with HTTP (not HTTPS) and fake domain
  6. Asking for password and card details via email

-------------------------------------------------------------------
From:    amazon-rewards@free-prizes.net       ← [🚩 NOT an Amazon domain]
To:      you@email.com
Subject: Congratulations! You Won $1,000 Gift Card 🎉 ← [🚩 TOO GOOD TO BE TRUE]

Dear Winner,                                  ← [🚩 GENERIC GREETING]

You have been SELECTED as our lucky winner!!
Clame your reward now befor it expiers!!!     ← [🚩 SPELLING ERRORS]

>> CLICK HERE TO CLAIM: http://amzn-giftcard-winner.xyz <<  ← [🚩 FAKE URL]

Hurry! Offer expires in 1 HOUR!               ← [🚩 ARTIFICIAL URGENCY]

Amazon Rewards Team

RED FLAGS FOUND: 5
  1. Sender not from @amazon.com
  2. Unrealistic prize offer
  3. Multiple spelling errors ("Clame", "befor", "expiers")
  4. Fake URL with misleading domain
  5. Artificial 1-hour countdown to pressure victim

SAMPLE EMAIL 3 — "IT Department" Internal Phishing
-------------------------------------------------------------------
From:    it-support@company-helpdesk.info     ← [🚩 NOT company's real domain]
To:      you@yourcompany.com
Subject: ACTION REQUIRED: Password Reset NOW  ← [🚩 URGENCY]

Hi,                                           ← [🚩 GENERIC GREETING]

Our system detected your password has been compromised.
You MUST reset your password immediately using the link below.

Reset Password: http://reset-yourcompany.info/login  ← [🚩 FAKE DOMAIN]

If you do not reset in 2 hours, your account will be locked. ← [🚩 THREAT]

— IT Support Team

NOTE: Do NOT forward this email to your IT department. ← [🚩 ISOLATION TACTIC]

RED FLAGS FOUND: 5
  1. Email not from official company domain
  2. ALL CAPS urgency in subject
  3. Threatening account lockout
  4. Suspicious external reset link
  5. "Do NOT forward" — designed to stop victim from verifying

SAMPLE EMAIL 4 — "Bank Alert" Phishing
-------------------------------------------------------------------
From:    alerts@sbi-secure-banking.com        ← [🚩 NOT sbi.co.in]
To:      you@email.com
Subject: Your SBI Account Blocked – Verify Immediately

Dear Valued Customer,

Your State Bank of India account has been temporarily blocked
due to multiple failed login attempts from an unknown device.

To unblock, click the link below and enter your:
  • Net banking User ID & Password              ← [🚩 CREDENTIAL REQUEST]
  • Date of Birth
  • ATM PIN                                     ← [🚩 PIN via email — NEVER legitimate]

>> Unblock Now: http://sbi-unblock-verify.xyz << ← [🚩 FAKE DOMAIN]

This link expires in 30 minutes.               ← [🚩 URGENCY]

SBI Customer Support

RED FLAGS FOUND: 5
  1. Domain is "sbi-secure-banking.com", not "sbi.co.in"
  2. Asks for ATM PIN — banks NEVER do this
  3. Asks for Net Banking password — banks NEVER do this
  4. Fake unblock link with suspicious domain
  5. 30-minute countdown creates false panic

SAMPLE EMAIL 5 — "HR Department" Phishing (Spear Phishing)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
From:    hr@yourcompany-hr.net               ← [🚩 LOOKALIKE DOMAIN]
To:      you@yourcompany.com
Subject: Salary Revision — Please Sign Document

Hi [Employee],

Please review and sign your updated salary revision letter
attached below before end of day.                ← [🚩 URGENCY — EOD deadline]

Attachment: Salary_Revision_2026.exe            ← [🚩 EXECUTABLE FILE .exe]

This is confidential. Do not share with colleagues. ← [🚩 ISOLATION TACTIC]

Best Regards,
Human Resources

RED FLAGS FOUND: 4
  1. HR domain is "yourcompany-hr.net" not the real company domain
  2. Attachment is an .exe file (executable — can install malware)
  3. "Do not share" instruction isolates the victim
  4. End-of-day pressure to act without thinking

KEY TAKEAWAYS:
  1. ALWAYS verify the sender's email domain — not just the display name.
  2. NEVER click links in unexpected emails — go directly to the website.
  3. NEVER enter a password, OTP, or PIN through an email link.
  4. If unsure, call the organization directly using their official number.
  5. Report phishing emails to your IT/security team immediately.


SECTION 5: HOW TO PROTECT YOURSELF
================================================================
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all accounts
  • Use a password manager to avoid reusing passwords
  • Install browser extensions like "Netcraft" to detect phishing sites
  • Keep your antivirus and OS updated
  • Train yourself with free tools: Google Phishing Quiz, PhishTank
